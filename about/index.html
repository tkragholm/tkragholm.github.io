About

I am a PhD student in register-based epidemiology at Mary Elizabeth’s Hospital, Rigshospitalet, Copenhagen University Hospital, and the University of Copenhagen. My thesis, Severe chronic disease in childhood, follows Danish children and their families through the national registers. It asks three questions about the same children: how their parents’ income develops after the diagnosis, what their healthcare costs over the following ten years, and how they fare in education, work and income up to age 30.

Much of the work is methods: building cohorts from register data, matching, survival and competing-risks models, and standard errors that can be reproduced. I write that code as open-source Rust: the epistat crates for the statistics, and sas7bdat-parser-rs, a reader for the SAS data files that Danish register data usually arrives in. The guides on this site explain the methods for clinicians, health economists and PhD students, with the code to run them.

Studies

  • Long-term Parental Income Trajectories in Families of Children with Severe Chronic Disease: A Danish Nationwide Population-based Cohort Study. Under review.
  • Excess Healthcare Costs over Ten Years in Children Diagnosed with Severe Chronic Disease Before Age Six: A Danish Nationwide Matched Cohort Study. In preparation.
  • Education, Employment and Income up to Age 30 After Severe Chronic Disease in Childhood: A Danish Nationwide Matched Cohort Study. Analysis in progress.
